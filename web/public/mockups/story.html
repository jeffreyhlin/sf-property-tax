SF Property Tax Gap

$2 billion a year, and a map of who keeps it

San Francisco taxes its homes as if they were worth $2.0 billion less than this model estimates they are. The law works that way on purpose, and the effect falls unevenly across the city.

How it works

The 1978 freeze

Prop 13 fixes a home's taxable value at its purchase price and lets it rise no more than 2% a year. Market prices ignore that ceiling.

Hold a home for thirty years and the difference between your bill and a new buyer's bill grows very large.

The map

Reading the colors

Deep orange marks homes taxed far below their estimated value. Pale marks homes close to market. The city turns orange toward the west, where owners tend to stay for decades.

Where the $2B sits

Most of it is people who never left

$1.5B held since before 2007
$0.4B bought and held since
$0.1B transfers with no reassessment

Long ownership accounts for most of the gap. The smaller slice covers homes that changed hands without a tax reset, and those are the ones the county's recorded deeds can settle.

The records

Every name is public

California has published recorded deeds for decades. Grantor, grantee, document type, and date all sit in the county index, and this map pulls them for whichever parcel you open.

That turns a guess about why a home is taxed low into something you can check.
